#### B. Pain Points

| Pain Point | Intensity (1–5) | Evidence |
|------------|----------------|----------|
| No clear structure for what to introduce and when | 5 | Nourishing Bubs provides week-by-week guide because parents ask for exactly this |
| Fear of missing a reaction or not knowing what counts as "a reaction" | 5 | Solid Starts dedicates a full section to what reactions look like; major parent anxiety driver |
| Not knowing allergen exposure must be maintained (not just introduced once) | 4 | Solid Starts addresses this explicitly as a common misunderstanding |
| Solid Starts is too expensive for a 4–6 month use window | 4 | App Store reviews; Trustpilot; paywall controversy caused community backlash |
| Reaction tracking is scattered (camera roll, notes app, memory) | 4 | No competitor has solved photo-based reaction tracking well |
| App logging bugs (Solid Starts: wrong-day entries, crashes) | 3 | Trustpilot and App Store complaints |
| Complexity of continuing allergen exposure (maintenance phase) | 3 | Nourishing Bubs dedicates full section; Solid Starts FAQ addresses it |

#### C. Trigger Events

| Trigger | Description | Implication for AllergenFirst |
|---------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **Pediatrician conversation** | Doctor says "you can start introducing allergens" with little guidance | This is the primary acquisition moment — reach parents at this trigger |
| **Baby turns 4–6 months** | Parent Googles "when to introduce allergens to baby" | ASO and content strategy should target this query |
| **First allergen introduction day** | "Today I'm doing peanuts" — high anxiety moment | Onboarding should reduce first-day anxiety with clear protocol guidance |
| **Mild skin reaction** | Baby gets a rash, parent panics and scrambles for records | Post-reaction tracking = pain point — clear in-app guidance is critical here |
| **Reading about LEAP study** | Parent discovers early introduction prevents allergies, wants to act | Educational moment → conversion opportunity |
| **Seeing "Solid Starts alternative" in a parenting group** | Mom mentions $100/year is too much; group searches for alternatives | ASO on "solid starts alternative" and community seeding strategy |

#### D. Desired Outcomes

| Outcome | "Don't want" | "Do want" |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Structure | To wing it and guess | A protocol that tells them what to do when |
| Confidence | To feel like they're missing something | To finish the intro period feeling like they did it right |
| Records | Scattered notes in camera roll | A clean timeline they can hand to a doctor |
| Cost | To pay $100/year for content they don't need | A focused tool at a fair one-time price |
| Emotional experience | Anxiety, notifications, pressure, optimization | Calm, simple, "check it off and move on" |

**Real language from web sources:**
- *"Introducing allergens to your baby can feel a bit overwhelming"* — Nourishing Bubs
- *"If you're feeling a bit nervous you can smear the teeniest bit on their lips first"* — Nourishing Bubs

**Implied language (from FAQ structure = questions parents actually ask):**
- "When do I start? Is my baby ready?"
- "What if I have a family history of allergies?"
- "What does a reaction actually look like?"
- "How long do I wait between allergens?"
- "Do I have to keep feeding allergens forever?"

**Language to use in AllergenFirst copy:**
- "Follow a plan" (not "track your data")
- "Reduce the guesswork" (not "optimize allergen introduction")
- "Records for your pediatrician" (not "doctor reports")
- "Stay organized" (not "log symptoms")
- "Introduce with confidence" (core emotional outcome)

### Jobs AllergenFirst is HIRED to do:
1. Replace the pediatrician's vague "start allergens" instruction with a day-by-day protocol
2. Replace scattered notes/camera roll with structured reaction logging
3. Replace anxiety with calm confidence at each introduction step
4. Replace "I had notes in my phone" at the doctor's office with a proper PDF report

### Jobs AllergenFirst is NOT hired to do:
- Replace a food database (Solid Starts owns this)
- Replace a general baby log (Huckleberry owns this)
- Diagnose allergic reactions (cannot and should not attempt)
- Replace the pediatrician relationship

### Hire/Fire Criteria
**Parents hire AllergenFirst when**: Starting the allergen introduction phase and feeling unprepared; had a reaction scare; found Solid Starts too expensive or too broad.

**Parents fire AllergenFirst when**: Allergen introduction phase is complete (natural churn — expected); app is confusing; they feel more anxious after using it than before.

## 3. Assumption Map

### Categorized by Risk × Impact

#### CRITICAL (High Risk, High Impact — must validate before building)

| # | Assumption | Why it might be wrong | Validation method |
|---|-----------|----------------------|------------------|
| C1 | Parents will pay $5–10 one-time for a focused allergen tracker | Free alternatives (AllyBaby) exist; parents may not see enough differentiation | Fake door test / landing page conversion |
| C2 | The 30-day introduction protocol is the key differentiator — not price alone | Baby Food Tracker and AllyBaby do logging without a protocol; unclear if parents want a protocol or just logging | Mom Test interviews asking how they currently structure introductions |
| C3 | Anxiety reduction is the primary emotional purchase driver (more than data completeness) | Some parents are data-motivated and want dashboards | Interview: ask what they felt when starting allergens, what would have made it easier |
| C4 | Parents will organize their workflow around a new specialized app rather than just use Solid Starts free tier | Habit switching is hard; Solid Starts has brand trust | Interview: ask what they currently use and why they would switch |

#### HIGH (Medium Risk, High Impact — validate early)

| # | Assumption | Why it might be wrong | Validation method |
|---|-----------|----------------------|------------------|
| H1 | Doctor-ready PDF export is a genuine purchase trigger (not just a nice-to-have) | Parents may not actually bring records to pediatrician visits | Ask in interviews: "Did you bring any food records to your last pediatrician visit? What did you bring?" |
| H2 | Photo-based skin reaction logging is a valued feature | Parents may just use their camera roll and that's good enough | Interview: "Show me how you documented the last reaction your baby had." |
| H3 | Primary discovery channel is parenting Reddit/Facebook communities | ASO search might be equally strong; word-of-mouth hard to seed as a solo dev | Test: Post helpful content in r/BabyLedWeaning; measure engagement and referral |
| H4 | The allergen introduction phase (4–10 months) is long enough to justify a purchase | Some parents may be past this phase; others may not reach it | Check: What % of target downloads come from parents in the 4–8 month window vs. outside |
| H5 | iOS is the right first platform | Android market might be larger in target markets | App Store vs. Google Play split in target demographics (US, AU, UK) |

#### MEDIUM (Lower Risk or Lower Impact — monitor, don't block on)

| # | Assumption | Why it might be wrong | Validation |
|---|-----------|----------------------|-----------|
| M1 | One-time purchase is preferred over low monthly subscription | Some parents prefer subscription so they can cancel after 4 months | A/B test pricing at launch |
| M2 | Offline-first privacy is a secondary purchase motivator | Privacy may be a stronger driver than assumed | Add to landing page and measure scroll/click on privacy section |
| M3 | No account required is a feature parents notice and value | Many parents are comfortable with accounts | Mention in onboarding and measure drop-off with/without |
| M4 | Multiple child profiles is post-MVP | Some parents have twins or older kids with sensitivity issues | Interview: Ask about household composition |

## 4. Experiment Designs

### Experiment 1: Validate willingness to pay (Critical Assumption C1)
**Target assumption**: Parents will pay $5–10 one-time for a focused allergen tracker  
**Hypothesis**: 15% of parents who see a landing page for AllergenFirst will click "Buy Now" at $7.99 one-time  
**Build**: A simple landing page (no app needed) with: headline, 3 feature bullets, pricing, a "Get it on App Store" button that leads to a waitlist form  
**Measure**: Click-through rate on "Buy Now" / waitlist signups  
**Learn**: If CTR > 10% → price is credible; if < 5% → revisit positioning or price point  
**Validation ladder level**: 4 (real commitment signal — entering email for waitlist)  
**Cost**: $0 (use Carrd or Notion; drive traffic via Reddit posts)  
**Timeline**: 2 weeks

### Experiment 2: Validate the protocol as the key hook (Critical Assumption C2)
**Target assumption**: Parents want a protocol (what to do when) more than just a log  
**Hypothesis**: Parents in BLW/parenting communities will engage more with "30-day allergen introduction plan" framing than "allergen tracker" framing  
**Build**: Two Reddit posts in r/BabyLedWeaning — one titled "I made a 30-day allergen introduction plan for my baby — sharing it for free" (protocol framing) vs. "I made a simple allergen reaction log — anyone want it?" (tracker framing)  
**Measure**: Upvotes, comments, DM requests for the free resource  
**Learn**: Which framing generates more organic demand and which generates more "this is exactly what I need" comments  
**Validation ladder level**: 3 (meaningful engagement, low commitment)  
**Cost**: $0  
**Timeline**: 1 week

### Experiment 3: Validate doctor-export as a purchase trigger (High Assumption H1)
**Target assumption**: PDF export for doctors is a genuine pull feature, not just a nice-to-have  
**Hypothesis**: 60% of interviewees will say they would want something to show their pediatrician when describing their allergen tracking workaround  
**Build**: 10 Mom Test interviews (phone/video) with parents who have babies 4–10 months old  
**Key Mom Test questions**:
- "Tell me about the last time you introduced a new allergen to your baby."
- "What did you bring to your last pediatrician visit? What did you wish you had?"
- "If your baby had a reaction, how would you document it right now?"
- "Has your pediatrician ever asked you what foods you've tried? What did you say?"
**Measure**: How many mention doctor/pediatrician unprompted; how many show pain when describing their current documentation  
**Learn**: If doctor-export comes up unprompted → strong signal; if only after prompting → it's a feature, not a trigger  
**Validation ladder level**: 3–4 (real behavior descriptions from real people)  
**Cost**: $0 (DM parents in Facebook "Baby-Led Weaning for Beginners" group or r/BabyLedWeaning)  
**Timeline**: 2–3 weeks

### Experiment 4: Test community seeding as acquisition channel (High Assumption H3)
**Target assumption**: Primary discovery will happen through parenting communities, not ASO  
**Hypothesis**: A helpful Reddit post with a free allergen introduction resource will generate 10+ DMs requesting the full product  
**Build**: Create a free, beautifully formatted 1-page PDF allergen introduction schedule (the "free version" of the 30-day plan) and post it in r/BabyLedWeaning  
**Measure**: Upvotes, comments, DM volume, waitlist signups from post  
**Learn**: Whether community seeding is a viable zero-cost acquisition channel at scale  
**Validation ladder level**: 3  
**Cost**: $0  
**Timeline**: 1 week (run alongside Experiment 2)

## 5. Mom Test Interview Guide

Use for Experiment 3. These questions pass The Mom Test — they ask about past behavior, not opinions about your idea.

**Opening (context)**
- "Can you tell me a bit about your baby — how old are they, and where are you at with introducing solid foods?"

**Problem discovery**
- "Walk me through the last time you introduced a new allergen. What did you do?"
- "What was going through your head the first time you gave your baby peanuts?"
- "What did you do to prepare for it?"

**Current behavior (solution mapping)**
- "How are you currently keeping track of what foods you've introduced?"
- "If your baby had a skin reaction after a meal, what would you do right now to document it?"
- "What would you bring to a pediatrician appointment if they asked about your baby's food history?"

**Pain intensity**
- "Has there been a moment where you thought 'I wish I had a better way to handle this'? Tell me about it."
- "Have you paid for any apps to help with starting solids? What made you pay / not pay?"

**Closing**
- "Is there anything about this process that keeps you up at night, or anything you feel like you're just winging?"

**Do NOT say**: "Would you use an app that..." / "What do you think of this idea..." / "Would you pay for..."
