## 2. Contribution Valuation Framework

### Methodology: Slicing Pie by Mike Moyer

The Slicing Pie model says: **your equity should equal your percentage of total risk taken**. It assigns market-rate values to each type of contribution, then calculates each person's share of the total.

**Multipliers used:**
- **Time (sweat equity):** 2x market salary — because working for free at a pre-revenue startup carries risk. Every $1 of salary you forgo is worth $2 in equity terms.
- **Cash invested:** 4x — cash is harder to get back than time, so it's valued higher.
- **Idea / IP:** Not separately valued. YC's position: ideas are worth almost nothing; execution is everything. James had the original idea, but all three founders execute.

### Market Rates (Vietnam Context)

Since all founders are based in Vietnam, we use **Vietnamese market rates** as the baseline for what each person's time is worth. This is fairer than using US rates, which would inflate numbers unrealistically.

| Role | Vietnam Monthly Rate (USD) | Annual Rate | Source |
|------|---------------------------|-------------|--------|
| Full-Stack Dev (React Native/Expo/Supabase) | $2,200 - $5,000 | $26,400 - $60,000 | Wellfound, Second Talent Vietnam |
| UI/UX Designer (senior) | $900 - $1,500 | $10,800 - $18,000 | Glassdoor Vietnam |
| Product Marketing Manager | $800 - $1,500 | $9,600 - $18,000 | Estimated from SEA benchmarks |

**For this analysis, I'll use mid-range estimates:**
- James (Dev/Product): **$3,500/mo = $42,000/yr**
- Ngoc (Design): **$1,200/mo = $14,400/yr**
- Vy (Product + Marketing): **$1,500/mo = $18,000/yr** (combined scope commands higher rate than pure marketing)

## 4. Proposed Scenarios

### Scenario A: Pure Math (Slicing Pie Result)

| Founder | Equity |
|---------|--------|
| James | 57% |
| Ngoc | 19% |
| Vy | 19% |
| Option Pool | 5% |

**Logic:** Follows the numbers exactly. James's equity stays roughly the same because he's done the most work. Ngoc gives up 11 points; Vy gains 4 points. 5% option pool set aside.

**Who gives what:** Ngoc gives 11% (30% → 19%). James gives nothing (-0%). Vy receives 4%.

**Pro:** Mathematically fair. Evidence-based.
**Con:** Ngoc takes the entire hit. May feel punitive even though the math supports it.

### Scenario B: Shared Adjustment (Recommended)

| Founder | Equity | Change |
|---------|--------|--------|
| James | 50% | -5% |
| Ngoc | 25% | -5% |
| Vy | 20% | +5% |
| Option Pool | 5% | New |

**Logic:** Both James and Ngoc contribute equally to rebalancing. This acknowledges that:
1. James's 55% was generous relative to a 3-person team (YC says equal splits perform better)
2. Ngoc's 30% was slightly high relative to actual contribution
3. Vy's expanded role deserves meaningful recognition
4. A 5% option pool protects the company for future hires/advisors

**Who gives what:** James gives 5%. Ngoc gives 5%. Together that funds Vy's increase (+5%) and the option pool (5%).

**Pro:** Nobody takes a disproportionate hit. James stays majority. Ngoc stays meaningful. Vy gets fair recognition. Option pool exists.
**Con:** Slightly generous to Ngoc vs. pure math. Slightly stingy to Vy vs. pure math.

---

### Scenario D: Dynamic/Slicing Pie (Ongoing)

Instead of a one-time restructure, adopt the Slicing Pie model going forward:

1. Set a **starting baseline** (e.g., Scenario B numbers)
2. Track each founder's monthly hours in a shared spreadsheet
3. Recalculate equity quarterly based on cumulative contribution
4. Lock the split at a **trigger event** (first revenue, first funding, or a set date like Dec 2026)

**Pro:** Most fair over time. No guessing about future contributions.
**Con:** More complex. Requires discipline to track. Can feel unstable.

## 5. My Recommendation

**Scenario B (Shared Adjustment)** is the strongest option for Petio right now. Here's why:

**From a financial advisor's perspective:**

1. **James at 50% is still controlling.** He built the product. He funded the expenses. 50% maintains his motivation and leadership authority. Dropping below 50% before any external funding would be unusual for the primary technical founder.

2. **Ngoc at 25% reflects reality.** The pure math says ~19%, but rounding up to 25% accounts for the fact that design is harder to quantify than code output. Good design drives conversion, retention, and brand — all critical for Petio's AI-first positioning. 25% is a fair compromise between math and strategic value.

3. **Vy at 20% is the minimum fair number.** She's taking on product + marketing — the two functions that determine whether Petio gets users and keeps them. At pre-revenue, growth is existential. 20% says "we value what you're building." If she delivers on the June launch and Vietnam growth plan, you could revisit toward 25% at the next milestone.

4. **5% option pool is prudent.** You don't plan to hire immediately, but you may need advisors, a content creator, or a part-time engineer. 5% gives you flexibility without over-diluting founders.

**What to avoid:**
- Don't give Vy less than 20%. At 15%, her expanded responsibilities aren't reflected. She'll feel like she's doing more work for the same equity — a recipe for resentment.
- Don't skip the option pool. Every investor will ask about it. Having it set up shows maturity.

## 6. Critical Next Steps (Non-Negotiable)

### 6.1 Formalize with a Vesting Schedule

**This is the single most important thing you can do.**

Your equity is currently verbal. This means:
- If any founder leaves tomorrow, they keep their equity forever
- No investor will fund you without a cap table and vesting agreement
- Any dispute has zero legal protection

**Standard vesting for co-founders:**
- **4-year vesting, 1-year cliff**
- Since you've been working ~10 months, consider **backdating the vesting start to June 2025** so everyone gets credit for time served
- After the cliff (June 2026 — conveniently near your launch), each founder has vested 25% of their allocation
- Remaining 75% vests monthly over 36 months

**Example for Scenario B (backdated to June 2025):**

| Founder | Total | Vested by Apr 2026 (10 mo) | Cliff (Jun 2026, 12 mo) | Fully Vested |
|---------|-------|---------------------------|------------------------|-------------|
| James | 50% | 0% (pre-cliff) | 12.5% | Jun 2029 |
| Ngoc | 25% | 0% (pre-cliff) | 6.25% | Jun 2029 |
| Vy | 20% | 0% (pre-cliff) | 5.0% | Jun 2029 |

### 6.2 Get It in Writing

You need a **Founders' Agreement** that covers:
- Equity percentages and vesting schedule
- What happens if someone leaves (unvested shares return to pool)
- IP assignment (all work belongs to Petio, not individuals)
- Decision-making rights (who has final say on what)
- Dispute resolution process

**Cost:** A basic founders' agreement in Vietnam costs $200-500 through a local legal service. In the US, services like Clerky or Stripe Atlas offer templated agreements for $500-1,000.

**Do this before the June launch.** Once you have users and revenue, restructuring becomes 10x harder.

### 6.3 Update company.md

After the team agrees, update `context/company.md` with the new equity split so the entire Startup OS reflects reality.

## 7. How to Have This Conversation

As a financial advisor, here's what I'd suggest for the actual discussion:

1. **Frame it as growth, not punishment.** "Vy's role has expanded. We need to update the equity to reflect that — and set up proper vesting before launch."

2. **Present the math first.** Show the Slicing Pie analysis. Let the numbers speak. Then propose Scenario B as a compromise.

3. **Emphasize vesting.** The real win is getting vesting in place. The exact percentages matter less than having a formal agreement that protects everyone.

4. **Give James context.** He's going from 55% to 50% — a 5-point drop. But he's gaining: a more motivated Vy, a proper cap table, and investor-readiness. Frame it as: "You're trading 5% for a company that's fundable."

5. **Give Ngoc context (yourself).** You're going from 30% to 25%. The math actually says ~19%, so 25% is generous. You're giving 5% to make the team sustainable.

6. **Make it time-bound.** "Let's agree on this split, set up vesting, and revisit at Series A or after 12 months of revenue — whichever comes first."
