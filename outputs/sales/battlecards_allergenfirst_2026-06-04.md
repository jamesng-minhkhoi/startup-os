# AllergenFirst — Competitive Battlecards
**Date**: 2026-06-04  
**Use for**: App Store listing copy, community responses, landing page objection handling

---

## Battlecard 1: vs. Solid Starts

### Their Pitch
"The most trusted app for starting solids — 400+ foods, expert guidance from pediatric feeding specialists, allergen introduction module included."

### Their Strengths
- Massive food database with prep videos and safety info
- Pediatrician and feeding therapist credibility
- Strong brand in BLW communities
- Allergen 101 module with serving size guidance

### Their Weaknesses
- $99.99/year — for a stage that lasts 4–6 months, that's $8–25 per month of actual use
- App is buggy: logging entries wrong day, crashes, unintuitive navigation
- Content platform with tracking bolted on — reaction logging is shallow
- Paywall controversy (2023) damaged community trust
- No photo-based skin reaction logging
- No structured day-by-day introduction schedule
- No offline / privacy-first option

### Our Counter
"Solid Starts is a great food education library. AllergenFirst is for parents who already know what to introduce and just need a structured plan, a clear reaction log, and something to show their pediatrician — without paying $100/year for content they won't use."

### Proof Points
- 30-day structured protocol vs. general "Allergens 101" module
- One-time $[X] vs. $99.99/year
- Photo reaction logging linked to food entries
- Offline-first, no account required
- Doctor-ready PDF export

### Objection Handling
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Solid Starts has pediatrician backing" | "AllergenFirst is reviewed by [pediatric allergist] and includes safety disclaimers at every step. For structure and logging, you don't need a 400-food database." |
| "Solid Starts does more" | "It does — but 90% of what you pay for is food prep content. If you need that, great. If you need allergen tracking, you're paying for things you'll never use." |
| "I already use Solid Starts" | "Keep it for the food database. Use AllergenFirst for the allergen introduction tracking — it's $[X] once, not $100/year." |

## Battlecard 2: vs. Baby Food & Allergy Tracker

### Their Pitch
"Privacy-first baby food and allergy tracker. No accounts, no servers. Your baby's data never leaves your phone."

### Their Strengths
- Best-in-class privacy architecture (local-only, no account, COPPA)
- 14-allergen tracker (more than top 9)
- PDF export (premium)
- Caregiver sync via browser (no app install for partner)
- Reasonable price vs. Solid Starts ($24.99/yr)

### Their Weaknesses
- No structured introduction protocol — it's a log, not a guide
- Key features locked behind subscription (PDF export, allergen tracker, multiple children)
- No photo-based reaction logging
- No reminder/scheduling system
- Low brand visibility — hard to discover
- $24.99/yr still a subscription; no one-time option

### Our Counter
"Baby Food Tracker has great privacy architecture. But it's a blank log — it won't tell you what to introduce, in what order, or what to watch for. AllergenFirst gives you the protocol, not just the clipboard."

### Proof Points
- Structured 30-day introduction plan (they have none)
- Photo logging for skin reactions (they have none)
- Built-in reminders tied to the introduction schedule
- One-time purchase, not a subscription — and our core features aren't paywalled

### Objection Handling
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Baby Food Tracker is cheaper ($25/yr)" | "AllergenFirst is $[X] once. You pay once, it's yours. No yearly renewal during an already expensive first year." |
| "It has 14 allergens vs. 9" | "The top 9 account for 90% of allergic reactions in infants. We focus where the risk is highest." |
| "It does caregiver sync" | "That's on our roadmap. For MVP: the most important person tracking allergen reactions is you — not a browser link." |

## Battlecard 3: vs. AllyBaby

### Their Pitch
"Free allergy tracker for parents — log foods, track symptoms, get an allergy risk score."

### Their Strengths
- Free (no purchase friction)
- Allergy risk scoring (0–10 automated scale)
- PDF export included free
- Multi-baby profiles
- 3-day observation windows built in

### Their Weaknesses
- Zero brand credibility — brand new, no reviews, unknown developer
- Free = sustainable only with ads or data monetization (trust risk)
- No structured introduction protocol
- Cloud sync = privacy concern
- No photo-based reaction logging
- No introduction scheduling or reminders
- Risk scoring could be confusing or misleading for anxious parents

### Our Counter
"AllyBaby is free, which sounds great — but free apps in the baby health space either run ads against your baby's health data or they add a paywall later. AllergenFirst is a one-time purchase from day one. No data sold. No surprises."

### Proof Points
- Clear business model (one-time purchase, no data monetization)
- Pediatric allergist content review (vs. unknown developer)
- Structured protocol vs. unstructured log
- No risk-scoring anxiety — we tell you what to watch for, not give your baby a score

### Objection Handling
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "AllyBaby is free" | "Free apps need to make money somehow. In baby health, that usually means ads or data. AllergenFirst costs $[X] once and makes money the clean way." |
| "AllyBaby has risk scoring" | "A 0–10 allergy score on a baby app created by an unknown developer isn't medical guidance — it's a liability. AllergenFirst helps you track clearly so your pediatrician can make the call." |
| "AllyBaby has more features" | "For now. New free apps without revenue usually either shut down or add a paywall. You'd lose your tracking history." |

## Battlecard 4: vs. "Paper / Notes App / Spreadsheet"

### Their Pitch
No pitch — it's what parents default to when no tool feels worth paying for.

### Their Strengths
- Free
- Already on the phone
- No learning curve
- Privacy (stays local)

### Their Weaknesses
- No structure — parents don't know what order to introduce allergens
- No reminders — easy to forget the 3-day wait window
- No doctor-ready export — "I have notes in my phone" is not useful at a pediatrician visit
- No reaction pattern visibility — can't easily see if two reactions were linked to the same food
- No photo organization — reaction photos are buried in camera roll

### Our Counter
"Paper notes don't have reminders, don't organize your reaction photos, and don't export a PDF your pediatrician can actually use. AllergenFirst is $[X] once — about the cost of one can of formula."
